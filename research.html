Research

I study how culture and human values are represented in artificial intelligence, how these systems shape human judgment and behavior, and how we can evaluate them across cultural and linguistic contexts. My work combines behavioral experiments, large-scale observational data, computational methods, and cross-cultural research.

AI, culture & evaluation

Research on how AI systems represent culture, values, and human judgment.

arXiv preprint · 2026

Accurate in Space, Unreliable in Time: How LLMs Represent National Cultural Change

Evaluates whether frontier LLMs capture not only where cultures are today, but how they have changed over time. The study compares more than two decades of World Values Survey data across 40 countries with cultural trajectories produced by four frontier LLMs.

Yalda Daryani, Miranda Bogen & Madeleine I. G. Daepp
Preprint PDF
PIBSS · 2026

The Homogenizing Engine: AI's Role in Standardizing Culture and the Path to Policy

Examines how LLMs may contribute to cultural homogenization through their representations, communication patterns, and effects on human behavior, and develops policy recommendations for preserving cultural diversity in AI systems.

Yalda Daryani, Zhivar Sourati & Morteza Dehghani

Findings of EMNLP · 2025

MFTCXplain: A Multilingual Benchmark Dataset for Evaluating the Moral Reasoning of LLMs

Introduces a multilingual benchmark containing 3,000 tweets in English, Persian, Portuguese, and Italian, annotated with hate speech labels, moral categories, and text-span rationales. The benchmark evaluates whether LLM outputs align with human moral reasoning.

Trager et al.

Trustworthy AI

Research on how trustworthiness can be measured and demonstrated in AI systems.

White Paper · 2026

Closing the AI Trust Gap: The Case for Independent Certification for Trustworthy AI

Examines the gap between responsible AI processes and independently verifiable trustworthy outcomes. The paper proposes independent, outcome-oriented certification as a mechanism for making trustworthiness measurable, comparable, and externally recognizable.

Papakonstantinou et al.

Social & cultural psychology

Research on culture, cognition, moral judgment, and social evaluation across diverse populations.

OSF Preprint · 2026

Asymmetric Moral Metaperceptions: Consequences for Political Polarization and Paths to Correction

Combining large-scale computational analysis with three behavioral experiments, including an intervention, we show that misperceptions of how political opponents view our morality shape trust and perceived threat and can be corrected.
